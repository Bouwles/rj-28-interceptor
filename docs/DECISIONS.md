# Decisions

One line per decision, newest at the bottom. "Brief" means the master build prompt.

| ID | Phase | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-001 | 0 | Blender 4.4.3 (the portable build already on this PC), not 5.2 LTS. All scripts target the 4.4 Python API. | The brief says ask before installing software. 4.4.3 does everything needed. |
| D-002 | 0 | The master build prompt stays local and is git-ignored. | The brief's layout puts it in the repo, but it names the inspirations, which rule 5 bans from repo text. |
| D-003 | 0 | Every script-facing number is in millimetres; geometry is stored in metres; the conversion happens once, in `obj_from_bm`. Objects keep identity transforms. | GLB exports at true scale and "apply all transforms" is true by construction. |
| D-004 | 0 | Bevels run on edge weights, not on angle. Panel gaps get their lip chamfer from the cutter profile. | An angle-limited bevel would try to bevel every boolean cut and collapse 1.5 mm grooves. |
| D-005 | 0 | Fasteners: one named part per set (copies of one master mesh joined), not one object per screw. | Keeps the part tree readable and the node count sane. Rules for size, spacing and counterbores live in `mech.fasteners`. |
| D-006 | 0 | The decal atlas is rendered inside Blender from text objects set in Saira Stencil One (OFL). | Reproducible from a clean checkout with no external rasteriser. |
| D-007 | 0 | Review renders use Cycles on OptiX throughout. | A frame takes one to three seconds on the RTX 4070 SUPER, and bevel highlights are trustworthy. |
| D-008 | 2 | Hand has six degrees of freedom: thumb opposition, thumb flexion, three finger flexions, one ganged finger splay. | The brief lists five and counts six. |
| D-009 | 2 | Knee actuators sit behind the thigh, either side of the centreline, driving a crank lug at the top rear of the shin (about 85 mm arm). | "One each side" on the outside of the knee would not fit between the legs at 240 mm hip spacing. |
| D-010 | 2 | The hip pitch actuator is a Ø174 drum forming the top of the thigh. | As outboard discs they read as ears on the pelvis. |
| D-011 | 2 | Thighs 212 mm and shins 190 mm wide, the most the hip spacing allows. | The first blockout had slim legs and read as a lab humanoid. |
| D-012 | 2 | Everything above the waist sits 30 mm forward of the hip axes; the ankle sits 10 mm further forward in the foot. | Backpack and cannon put the mass centre near the heels otherwise. A pure translation keeps the orthographic views square. |
| D-013 | 2 | Cap crown is about 101 mm tall, not 110. | Slit centre (1,620) and crown top (1,730) are fixed, and the cap's front edge has to clear the slit. |
| D-014 | 2 | Mount is a swing arm: trunnion at z = 1,515 mm behind the right shoulder, bore 125 mm above it. | With the trunnion through the breech the stowed cannon stood beside the head, hid the cap brim in side silhouette and was 2,300 mm tall. |
| D-015 | 2 | Barrel 500 mm; stowed overall height measures 2,115 mm. | The brief asks for a 750 mm barrel and about 2,050 mm overall. With the barrel level above the shoulder armour those cannot both hold. 440 mm gives 2,055; 750 gives 2,365. Variants rendered for G1. |
| D-016 | 2 | Yoke has one cheek, outboard. Cradle (guide rods, recoil unit) rides on top of the barrel. Feed box is the only thing inboard of the breech. | Keeps the head side clear through ±25° traverse and the underside clear of the shoulder armour. |
| D-017 | 2 | Right pauldron is 12 mm lower than the left and cut back at the rear to y = −10 mm. Left is larger: 356 mm against 344 mm from the centreline, longer skirt, rear cheek. | Clears the barrel at −5° and the pivot housing in traverse. The brief asks for this asymmetry. |
| D-018 | 2 | Faceplate is 148 mm wide at the brow with 14 mm corner chamfers. | Barrel clearance to the head at full inboard traverse was 3.5 mm with a 160 mm plate. |
| D-019 | 2 | The sweep check covers elevation −5° to 90° at zero traverse and traverse ±25° at both 0° and −5°. | More than the brief asks (it asks for traverse at 0° only). The mount should not need a software interlock to avoid its own robot. |
| D-020 | 2 | GitHub repository created at Phase 2 as private; it goes public at Phase 12 for Pages. | Paul asked for the repository now. The brief schedules publication after G3. |
| D-021 | G1 | Paul approved the blockout with four changes, all "match the concept art": stance about 425 mm between the feet (hip axes 270 mm, legs abducted 5.5°), feet 330 × 195 mm, shoulder armour 780 mm over 520 mm shoulder axes, barrel 455 mm. | Gate G1 outcome. Stowed height now measures 2,050 mm. |
| D-022 | 3 | Breech shortened to 190 × 164 × 176 mm. | With a 455 mm barrel the brief's breech made the cannon read as a box with a lens. |
| D-023 | 3 | After G1 Paul asked for far more intricacy, to the level of the concept art. Detail target raised: every assembly carries its real mechanism, carriers, fasteners and harness; part count is allowed past 500. | Paul's feedback on seeing the blockout. |
| D-024 | 3 | Armour is built as one rounded-section shell per limb segment, opened where mechanism shows, sliced into panels by 2 mm gap cuts, and lined with carbon ribs on aluminium standoffs. | One method for all seventeen assemblies; gives real thickness, even gaps and visible structure behind them. |
| D-025 | 3 | Knee: fork with an open corridor, crank pin 70 mm from the axis, phased so the lever arm is 40 mm at both ends of a 0 to 110° range and 70 mm in the middle; 115 mm stroke. Knee range is 110°, not 120°. | The first crank lost its lever arm near 90° because the actuator line passed through the joint centre. |
| D-026 | 3 | Neck: roll ears hang from the head and swing into the bore of the yaw ring. Ranges: yaw ±70°, pitch ±15°, roll ±12°. | Only 55 mm of height between the thorax deck and the skull. |
| D-027 | 3 | Sensor glass is transmissive (smoked), not opaque. | The slit emitter and the cameras have to be visible behind the window. |
| D-028 | 3 | Edge wear comes from a bevel-normal mask restricted to convex edges, not from pointiness alone. | Pointiness marked every smoothly curved panel as an edge and blotched the cap. |
| D-029 | 3 | The energy chain is one mesh with a shape key between the level and stowed poses; `poses.set_mount` drives it. | It links the elevating breech to the fixed backpack, so it has to deform; a morph target exports to glTF. |
| D-030 | 3 | Hero rig keeps the rim lights off the floor with light linking. | They pooled on the floor in front of the robot. |
| D-031 | 3 | Exploded view is two stages on a parent Empty per assembly: the Empty carries the stage 1 offset, each part its stage 2 offset, radial from the assembly's spine line. | Matches the brief's two stages and gives removal-direction motion instead of a blanket scale-up. |
| D-032 | 3 | Battery service door is on the backpack's left side panel, not the rear. | The rear face is taken by two blowers, two radiator grilles and the sight glass. |
| D-033 | 5 | Battery enclosure enlarged from 5.7 to 8.3 litres: a stepped casting with a foot, a saddle over the waist reaction beam, a rear tower behind the compute stack and low wings under the shoulder actuators. Cells are silicon-anode, stacked cell-to-pack. | The mass roll-up showed the first enclosure could hold about half of a 23 kg, 5.0 kWh pack. Now about 600 Wh/L and 2.8 kg/L at pack level, stated in the endurance note. |
| D-034 | 5 | Hip and waist actuator leads run to a connector panel on the back of the pelvis; each leg trunk starts at a plug on that panel. | Actuator ports were left without cables, and the leg looms started in mid-air. |
| D-035 | 6 | The hero light rig is laid out for a camera in front and turned about Z to wherever the camera is; two low-power strip boxes sit beside the lens. | With a fixed rig the rim lights became key lights in rear shots and burnt black armour to light grey. |
| D-036 | 6 | Part-to-part variation: armour, aluminium, titanium, carbon and rubber each take a small roughness and value offset per part. Decal white is a 56% paint grey and decal cyan is 62% of the emitter colour. | A fresh-eyes review read the model as two uniform materials, and the decals as glowing. |
| D-037 | 6 | Gear teeth are true 20° involutes with a relieved root; bellows have rounded crests and beaded cuffs. | The same review called the first versions clip-art gears and knife-edged bellows. |
| D-038 | 6 | Shoulder armour is carried on the roll housing, not the yoke: three plates (cap, main plate, hinged skirt) on a carrier. Abduction stops about 15° beyond rest, where the yaw housing meets the main plate. | The single hood read as a blank box. Fixing the plates to the roll housing is what lets the skirt hinge mean something; the cost is abduction range. |
| D-039 | 6 | Knee flexion is limited to 105° in use. The joint and crank allow 110°, but the knee actuator bodies meet the ankle actuators at about 106°. | Found when the rear leg guards were added and the leg was swept one degree at a time. |
| D-040 | 6 | The neck connector sits 24 mm right of the centreline under the skull; the waist yaw drive is cabled up to the thorax floor, not down to the pelvis panel. | The connector stood inside the neck yaw motor. The yaw drive rides on the turntable, so a lead to the pelvis would have crossed the joint it drives. |
| D-041 | 7 | The knee actuators pivot in fork ears machined into the thigh frame, on an anchor block that stands between the hip pitch housing and the sweep of the actuator bodies. The catalogue clevis is dropped at that end. | The first anchor was a block under the pivot: the clevis base was buried in it and the actuator bodies ran 18 mm through it. A catalogue base 58 mm wide cannot sit anywhere within reach of that pin without meeting the hip housing or the body. |
| D-042 | 7 | Knee actuator power ports are turned 135° into the valley behind and between the two bodies and staggered; ankle ports face each other across the gap, staggered. Leads follow the valley. `mech.linear_actuator(port=...)` sets position, turn and stand-off. | Forward ports put the connectors inside the thigh crosshead and the shin beam. The valley is the only free space inside the rear guards. |
| D-043 | 7 | Limb looms are bundles of two to four braided cables with a slow lay, bound by ties (`H.loom`); clips are sized to the bundle. The shin channel is 12 mm deep with open mouths beyond its cover, and the looms go into it. | Single fat tubes read as hoses. The first channel was 6 mm deep and the loom ended in the air outside its cover. |
| D-044 | 7 | Clevis bases carry two cap screws between the ears; ankle clevises sit on raised pads sized from `mech.clevis_seat`. | Nothing visibly held a clevis to its structure. |
| D-045 | 8 | No UV unwrap and no texture bake. Bevels are exported as real geometry, the fourteen materials are shared constant-factor stand-ins named `RJ28_<key>`, the web shader adds the fine texture, and each vertex carries masks in COLOR_0. The decal atlas is the only texture; it ships as WebP (2,048 px in LOD0, 1,024 px in LOD1), no KTX2. | 926 parts on 14 materials, measured at 1.92 M triangles with full bevels, 1.43 M at one segment, 0.97 M without. Eighteen 4K maps would not fit a 30 MB budget, and there is under 0.4 MB of texture to compress. |
| D-046 | 8 | LOD0: bevels at one segment with weighted normals, faces coplanar to within 0.2° merged, bevels narrower than 0.35 mm dropped. Nothing is decimated. 1,318,632 triangles, 14.4 MB. | The fastener sets and the harness carry no bevel modifier, so switching theirs off saved nothing; the coplanar merge gave 4.6% and the sub-pixel bevels 3%. Getting to 1.2 M would have meant decimating screws or dropping 0.4 mm bevels that do show in the close stations. |
| D-047 | 8 | LOD1: no bevels, faces merged to 1°, collapse decimation per part by material (aluminium, titanium, steel 14%; rubber 11%; braid 16%; cap 26%), floors of 8 triangles per screw, 36 per loose piece and 120 per part, weighted normals recomputed after. Armour is not decimated. Markings are re-seated on their hosts. 192,719 triangles, 3.4 MB. | With armour at 50% the check render showed a light panel between the thighs that LOD0 does not have, and it went when armour was left alone; markings float 0.12 mm above their hosts, less than a decimated surface moves. Armour is the silhouette, so the triangles come out of mechanism and fasteners. |
| D-048 | 8 | COLOR_0 is data. R = 1 on the faces of convex bevels and chamfers, set per face corner; G = 0; B = cavity, short-range occlusion cast about the corner's shading normal against the assembled robot and faded out on faces wider than about 70 mm. LOD1 has no bevels, so its R is 0. | A per-vertex edge mask cannot be 0 on a flat: every vertex of a bevelled flat sits on a bevel. Per corner costs vertex splits (1.17 M vertices for 1.32 M triangles). The first cavity pass used vertex normals and darkened whole panels from their lapped edges. |
| D-049 | 8 | Part nodes keep an identity transform and positions stay 32-bit floats, snapped to 1/32768 m. Normals are 12-bit in LOD0 and 8-bit in LOD1. All compressed streams of one layout share a buffer view. | Ordinary position quantisation hides a scale and an offset in every node, and the site sets node matrices itself for the mount and the exploded view. Twelve-bit normals cost 2.7 MB over eight in LOD0; one buffer view per accessor cost 0.8 MB of JSON per file. |
| D-050 | 8 | The GLBs are in the stowed pose. Mount parts carry the pose as their node matrix over meshes drawn level; the energy chain is a morph target; `mount.json` gives pivots, axes, the matrix rule and check matrices. Explode offsets of mount parts are measured with the mount level and applied inside the pose matrix. | `explode.apply()` measures parts where they stand against a cannon spine that is drawn level. Run with the mount stowed it gives the cannon parts offsets of up to 290 mm in the wrong direction; `parts.json` already holds the level values. |
| D-051 | 8 | The `AXIS_` empties are exported under a node `AXES`, turned so the node's local +Z is the joint axis in glTF; origin and axis are also in the node extras. The tracker tilt axis is posed with the cannon. | The exporter's change to +Y up would otherwise have left the axis on local +Y. |
| D-052 | 8 | Feature edges are a separate GLB of line segments taken from the un-bevelled meshes: folds over 30.5°, open boundaries, edges with more than two faces. Segments are ordered end to end before compression. Markings get a node and no lines. | Bevel weights were set at 30°, so this is the model's own definition of a hard edge. Ordering took the file from 3.4 to 2.7 MB. |
| D-053 | 8 | Tour stations: the mount stays stowed until station 06, swings to deployed across it, stays deployed for 07 and returns for 08 and 09. Moving stations carry `move`: `orbit` or `dolly`. | The general arrangement drawing that 08 cross-fades to is stowed. A straight line between the ends of a quarter orbit passes 30% closer to the robot than either end. |
| D-054 | 8 | `hero.hdri()` turns on camera visibility for the studio lights while it renders the panorama, and the export refuses a panorama with no lamp in it. | Lights are hidden from camera rays by default. The first studio.hdr was not black, but all it held was the lit floor: it would have lit the web model from below. |
| D-060 | 8 | The drawing sheets come from a projector written for the job (`drawings.py`): feature edges of the un-bevelled meshes, projected, with visibility decided by ray-casting each sample towards the viewer through a BVH of the scene. Freestyle and Line Art are not used. | It runs headless in a few seconds per view, gives the same lines every time, and keeps the lines grouped per part, which the site needs for "show in drawing". |
| D-061 | 8 | A visible edge is drawn thick when the surface behind it drops away by more than 2.6 mm on paper (13 mm at 1:5), thin otherwise. Whether the edge is a crease or a contour does not decide its weight. | With 926 parts every part has its own silhouette. Thick outlines per part turned the general arrangement black; a depth step picks out limbs against the body and leaves panel lines and screws thin. |
| D-062 | 8 | Faces seen edge-on are left out of the occluders for that view. | A ray that starts on an edge and runs along a face it touches hits or misses that face by rounding error, which shows as broken lines. An edge-on face covers no area in the view, so nothing is lost. |
| D-063 | 8 | Third-angle projection throughout. Views are named by the robot's side, not the observer's: on sheet 1 the left-hand side view is the robot's left and sits to the right of the front view. | The brief asks for one convention held consistently; naming by the machine's side matches every other document in the project. |
| D-064 | 8 | Scales: 1:5 general arrangement; 1:2 head views with the section at 1:1 and the slit at 2:1; 1:2.5 mount; 1:2 leg with details at 1:1; 1:6 exploded view. | 1:2.5 and 1:6 are not preferred scales. They are what lets three views of the mount stay in projection, and the exploded view fill the sheet, on A1. |
| D-065 | 8 | Mount clearances are shown as numbered flags keyed to a schedule taken from the sweep report, not as drawn dimensions. | Each minimum occurs at an oblique pose (for example −5° elevation, +7.5° traverse) and is not a true length in any of the three views. A dimension line there would be false. |
| D-066 | 8 | The leg sheet is drawn along and square to the knee axis, not in the robot's axes. | The leg is abducted 5.5°. Only in these views are the actuator pin centres, the crank radius and the moment arms true lengths. |
| D-067 | 8 | The exploded sheet moves each assembly root by its stage 1 offset itself. It does not call `explode.pose(0.5)`. | `explode.pose` also sets every part's location to its stage 2 offset, zero at that point, which wipes the translation in the stowed mount pose: the cannon was drawn half a metre from its yoke. |
| D-068 | 8 | Loose islands of a part smaller than 2.2 mm on paper are drawn by their contour only and dropped below 0.35 mm; harness and braid are drawn by contour only; markings are not drawn. | Keeps a screw set readable as circles at 1:5 and the largest sheet at 1.6 MB. Braid crease lines read as hatching. |
| D-069 | 8 | PNG previews and the PDF set are made by the Microsoft Edge already on the machine, run headless on the SVG files. | Nothing is installed. The PDF keeps the line work as vectors and the text as text. |
| D-070 | 8 | The right upper-arm plate carries the flag of Lebanon in its own colours, toned to paint. Decals can now be pictures (`F.label(aspect=...)`, drawn into the atlas by `markings.PICTURES`). | Paul asked for it opposite the RJ-28 designation. The right shoulder plates are cut back for the mount and have no clear field, so it sits on the plate below them. It is the one exception to cyan-only colour. |
| D-071 | 8 | Every part has a `strip` stage in parts.json (1 outer armour, 2 armour panels, 3 carriers and covers, 4 harness and boots, 0 stays). Screws, hinges, latches and markings take the stage of the outermost removable part they sit on, found by contact. | Paul asked for the armour to come off in layers on the site. The order has to come from the model, not from a list typed into the UI. |
| D-072 | 8 | Woven screens take their mass from area at wire gauge, not from enclosed volume. | An open sheet has no volume; the cap's arch mesh had come out at 2 kg. |
| D-073 | 9 | The site moves the robot on a rig written from the model (`build.py rig` → `rig.json`): a tree of 70 links on the 46 joint axes, every part assigned to the link it is bolted to, by name. | The GLB stays one node per part; nothing is skinned or re-exported. Pivots come from `axes.py`, the hand's own knuckle chain and the measured actuator pins. |
| D-074 | 9 | Linear actuators are solved, not keyed: body pinned on one link, rod on the other, kept in line as the joint turns, bellows stretched between. Cables, hoses and boots bend across the joints they span; lames and the knee guard take a share of their joint. | With the armour off the mechanism is what is on show; a rod that left its clevis would be the first thing seen. |
| D-075 | 9 | The feet stay where they stand: leg angles move the pelvis. Movements are keyed joint angles eased with a minimum-jerk profile and always return to rest. | No balance or contact solver is needed for movements made on the spot. |
| D-076 | 9 | Intercept: the body turns at the waist to bring the cannon on; traverse stays on centre above level, as the controls hold it. Missile, shot and burst are drawn on the clip's clock and kept out of the floor mirror. | The clearance sweep covered traverse only at level and below (D-065). |
